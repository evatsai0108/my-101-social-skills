1. Resource Type and Educational Stage

This web resource supports elementary social skills teaching. Teachers select lessons and supports according to learners’ communication, understanding, sensory and emotional needs, and use them alongside real activities.

Curriculum codes are planning references. Teachers should check formal lesson plans and IEP goals against the applicable curriculum, individual abilities and school procedures.

2. Resource Name

My 101 Social Skills Lessons

The site brings together planning, classroom presentation, illustrated worksheets, parent communication drafts and island practice. Traditional Chinese is the initial default; English is available, and the browser remembers the last choice.

3. Learners and Support

Designed around the needs of an elementary middle-grade social skills group, with teacher guidance. Learners may respond through speech, pictures, gestures or watching first, and may ask for rest or help.

Yu, He, Jia and Jun are example aliases and theatre characters, not actual learners or fixed diagnoses. Roles may rotate. Choose visual steps, short phrases, read-aloud support, reduced workload and breaks according to individual needs.

Participation cards offer help, repetition, observation, rest, another idea and readiness. The teacher checks the need and agrees on a next step; rest or refusal does not cost stars.

4. Why This Resource Was Made

Teachers often gather stories, pictures, activities and worksheets separately while trying to connect classroom practice with school and home life. This site keeps one lesson’s materials together to reduce switching and preparation time.

The aim is useful communication, cooperation, help-seeking and self-protection in daily life. After an activity, practise with different people and settings, listen to the learner and record the support needed.

5. Content and Development

The 101 lessons comprise 52 weekly lessons and 49 situation-based inspiration lessons. Each offers a goal summary, story, three steps, role practice and a worksheet. Teachers may adjust the order and amount.

The suggested 40-minute sequence is story, 00–08; skill steps, 08–16; role practice, 16–26; application, 26–36; and reflection, 36–40. It is flexible and does not require every learner to finish at the same pace.

The five companion books include a B4 teacher manual, B4 phonetic worksheets, and A4 colour worksheets, stories and skills, and role practice resources. Online contents link to each lesson; Chinese PDFs are compiled from the same updated sources.

The design provides varied representation, expression and participation through read-aloud support, pictures, step-by-step rehearsal and participation cards. Stories and choices are teaching materials. The site does not claim certification under a specific Social Stories version, verified accessibility conformance or psychological treatment.

Antigravity and Codex mainly assisted with coding and interface work. The teacher sets the teaching direction, reviews content and revises it through use. This description includes no unsupported development timeline, experimental percentages or individual progress figures.

Connecting Goals, Activities and Observation

For example, when joining a game: notice a pause, ask through speech or pictures, then check the response and next step. The lesson card, presentation and worksheet share these steps. The teacher separately observes independent use under the same goal and conditions.

6. Teaching and Assessment

Before class, check the goal, materials and learners’ readiness. Discuss the story, model the three steps and take turns rehearsing. Role practice initially shows one character, with the full group available when needed. End by asking who to practise with and where.

Teaching Observations records date, alias, setting, action, prompts and outcome. Rehearsal, school and home feedback are separate, as are independent and supported completion. No opportunity or a chosen break is excluded from the denominator; zero opportunities shows no data.

Worksheet ticks record practice. Stars, taught lessons, island completion and certificates record participation or teaching history; they do not determine IEP mastery. Different targets are not combined. Formal decisions require individual baselines and continuing observation.

Parent and IEP drafts use observations for the selected alias and date, leaving missing evidence pending. The teacher edits and reviews before copying or printing practice slips. Families report opportunities and support needs; signatures alone do not show learning.

7. Using the Site

Use a connected browser and test text size, sound and controls on the actual classroom device. Fonts, narration and scene assets may need the internet. Full offline operation and identical behaviour across all browser versions are not guaranteed.

On phones, expand Teaching Tools and Observations; the calm space remains directly available. Low stimulation is on by default to reduce unnecessary animation and sound. Deliberately selected narration and background sound remain user-controlled.

The calm space starts with natural breathing without requiring timed compliance. Learners may pause, watch or rest. The 4-7-8 and sensory activities are optional; stop if uncomfortable. A timer ending does not require an immediate return to class.

Stars, taught lessons, custom lessons, answers and observations are stored in the local browser. Observations export as JSON/CSV; island progress uses its own save code. These backups are separate. Keep originals and backups before changing devices, using private browsing or clearing site data.

Custom lessons require three practical steps, which link to their own presentation and practice template. They are not automatically added to the 101-lesson publications. Teachers still add questions and check suitability.
